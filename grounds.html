Stadio Diego Armando Maradona · Naples · Italy

SSC Napoli · opened 1959 · capacity 54,726

  • Maradona 1

  • Napoli 2–3 Real Madrid·Champions League, group stage·3 October 2023
  • Napoli 2–1 Juventus·Serie A·25 January 2025

the ground

  • Opened December 1959 as the Stadio del Sole. The first match played in it was Napoli beating Juventus 2–1.
  • Known as the San Paolo for sixty years. Renamed for Maradona days after his death in November 2020.
  • Napoli's first two league titles, in 1987 and 1990, were won with Maradona.
  • At the 1990 World Cup semi-final here, Maradona asked Neapolitans to consider whether Italy had ever cared for them, and to support Argentina. Italy lost on penalties.

october 2023

  • Napoli were the reigning champions of Italy, in their first home tie of the campaign. Rudi Garcia had taken the job over the summer.
  • Leo Østigård headed them in front on 19 minutes, from a corner Kepa came for and missed. Vinícius Júnior and Jude Bellingham had turned it round within a quarter of an hour.
  • Piotr Zieliński equalised from the penalty spot just before the hour, given for handball on a VAR check.
  • The winner came off the crossbar. Federico Valverde struck it from distance, it beat Meret, came back off the bar and went in off him — recorded as an own goal, which Ancelotti said afterwards seemed absurd.
  • It ended a run of thirteen home matches unbeaten in the competition.
  • Garcia was sacked five weeks later. Napoli finished tenth. Real Madrid won the tournament, their fifteenth.

january 2025

  • Napoli came from behind.
  • At the end of the night they were third, nine points behind Inter.
  • They had finished tenth the season before. Antonio Conte had previously managed Juventus.
  • They went on to win a fourth league title, their second in three years.
  • Scott McTominay, released by Manchester United the previous summer, was the outstanding player of the season.

Easter Road · Edinburgh · Scotland

Hibernian · opened 1893 · capacity 20,421

  • Easter Road 1
  • Easter Road 2
  • Hibernian 2–1 Rangers·Scottish Championship·1 November 2015

the ground

  • Record attendance 65,860, against Hearts, 2 January 1950.
  • Hibernian had last won the Scottish Cup in 1902.

november 2015

  • Both clubs were in the second tier. Rangers were top, unbeaten in eleven, and had won every league match under Mark Warburton until this one.
  • Jason Cummings scored from a David Gray throw-in. Rangers equalised through an own goal by Darren McGregor, a former Rangers player. Paul Hanlon headed the winner.
  • Hibernian did not go up that season. They lost the play-off.

what it led to

  • The two met again in the Scottish Cup final at Hampden on 21 May 2016.
  • Hibernian won 3–2. David Gray, the club captain, headed the winner in stoppage time.
  • It ended a wait of 114 years.

Estadio Monumental · Buenos Aires · Argentina

River Plate · opened 1938 · capacity 85,018

  • Monumental 1
  • Monumental 2
  • Monumental 3
  • River Plate 0–1 Gimnasia y Esgrima La Plata·Torneo Clausura·2 November 2025

the ground

  • Argentina won the 1978 World Cup here, in the third year of the dictatorship.
  • The Navy Mechanics School, where people were being held and killed at the time, stands a little over a kilometre away on the same avenue. Survivors have described hearing the noise of the stadium from inside the building.
  • The tournament was a deliberate exercise in national presentation, and the images it produced are the images of Argentina in 1978 that circulate.

november 2025

  • Every player was whistled as his name was read out before kick-off.
  • River had already been knocked out of the Copa Argentina and the Copa Libertadores. The superclásico was the following week.
  • Gimnasia scored from a penalty in the second half — their first goal in this stadium in twenty years.
  • In the sixteenth minute of stoppage time River were awarded a penalty. Miguel Borja's was saved.

Allianz Parque · Sao Paulo · Brazil

Palmeiras · opened 2014 · capacity 43,713

  • Allianz Parque 1
  • Allianz Parque 2
  • Allianz Parque 3
  • Palmeiras 0–0 Cruzeiro·Série A·26 October 2025

the ground

  • Built on the site of the Parque Antártica. The club was founded by Italian immigrants as Palestra Itália.
  • In 1942 Brazil entered the war against the Axis, clubs with enemy names were required to change them, and Palestra Itália became Palmeiras.

october 2025

  • Goalless, and the loudest place I have stood.
  • A challenge in the eleventh minute was reviewed and punished with a yellow card, and the crowd spent the rest of the match furious about it.
  • A bottle was thrown and hit the Cruzeiro goalkeeper, Cássio.
  • Palmeiras were top of the league that night by one point.

what happened next

  • Three days later they were three goals down to LDU Quito in a Libertadores semi-final. They won the second leg 4–0 and reached the final.
  • Flamengo beat them 1–0 in Lima, and had already taken the league by six points.

Marakana · Belgrade · Serbia

Crvena zvezda · opened 1963 · capacity 51,755

  • Marakana 1
  • Marakana 2
  • Marakana 3
  • Crvena zvezda 1–1 Vojvodina·SuperLiga, championship play-off·11 May 2025

the ground

  • Named, informally and permanently, after the Maracanã.
  • Capacity has fallen by more than half from the six figures it once claimed.

1991

  • Red Star drew 2–2 with Bayern Munich here in the semi-final, going through on an own goal in the last minute.
  • They beat Marseille on penalties in Bari on 29 May, the only Yugoslav club ever to win the European Cup.
  • Slovenia declared independence four weeks later.
  • The champions of Europe were barred from playing home matches in Belgrade, and gave up the trophy in Sofia. Yugoslav clubs were banned from European competition in 1992.
  • The Delije were formed in 1989. Their leader, Arkan, took members of the group to Croatia as the Serbian Volunteer Guard.

may 2025

  • The season was already decided. Red Star finished on 96 points, Vojvodina fourth on 52.
  • Early goal, late equaliser, low crowd, flares throughout.
  • The two met again in the cup final eleven days later, which Red Star won.
  • It was Red Star's 150th consecutive home league match without defeat. The run began in April 2017, and the last team to beat them at home was Vojvodina.

Bramall Lane · Sheffield · England

Sheffield United · opened 1855 · capacity 32,050

  • Sheffield United 3–1 Sheffield Wednesday·First Division·17 January 2003
  • Sheffield United 1–0 Arsenal·Premier League·30 December 2006
  • Sheffield United 2–2 Manchester United·FA Youth Cup final, first leg·17 May 2011
  • Sheffield United 2–2 Tottenham Hotspur·League Cup semi-final, second leg·28 January 2015
  • Netherlands 1–1 Sweden·Women's Euro 2022, group stage·9 July 2022
  • Sheffield United 2–0 West Bromwich Albion·Championship·26 April 2023

the ground

  • Leased as a cricket field in 1855.
  • First football match 1862, Hallam against Sheffield Club, four years before Sheffield United existed.
  • Hosted the first floodlit football match ever played, 14 October 1878, lit by two generators, 20,000 present.
  • Yorkshire played first-class cricket there until August 1973.
  • The oldest major ground in the world still staging professional football.

january 2003

  • Beat Liverpool in the first leg of a League Cup semi-final. Lost the second leg at Anfield in extra time, 3–2 on aggregate.
  • Beat Wednesday nine days later, in the 220th Sheffield derby.
  • That season United reached the League Cup semi-final, the FA Cup semi-final and the play-off final, and lost all three.
  • A Blades supporter had been shot dead in Walkley on New Year's Day. Wednesday fans were searched for banners about it and police leave was cancelled across the force.
  • Alan Quinn scored for Wednesday that night. He later scored for United in the derby, in December 2005, and remains the only man to have scored for both sides in the fixture.

december 2006

  • Christian Nadé's winner came in his third Premier League start.
  • Paddy Kenny pulled a muscle taking a goal kick. There was no reserve goalkeeper on the bench.
  • Phil Jagielka spent half an hour in goal and saved from Robin van Persie.
  • The result ended a seven-match unbeaten run for Arsenal.
  • On the final afternoon of the same season, Jagielka handled in his own area against Wigan. David Unsworth, a United player at the start of that season, scored the penalty. United were relegated.

may 2011

  • The first leg of the FA Youth Cup final.
  • 29,977 attended — around 9,000 more than the first team were averaging.
  • The first team were relegated to League One that season, having lost 26 of 46 matches.
  • Manchester United's side included Paul Pogba, Jesse Lingard, Ravel Morrison, Michael Keane, Will Keane and Sam Johnstone.
  • Lingard's opener was a deflected shot from a Pogba cross, given despite Harry Maguire heading it out from under his own bar.
  • United lost the second leg 4–1, 6–3 on aggregate. Maguire was concussed at Old Trafford, stretchered off, and spent the night in hospital.

january 2015

  • A League One side against Tottenham, in snow and hail.
  • Ché Adams, eighteen, had been signed out of non-league Ilkeston three months earlier.
  • He came off the bench and scored twice in three minutes to level the tie.
  • Christian Eriksen scored his second of the night in the 88th minute.
  • United had beaten five Premier League clubs in the preceding year.

july 2022

  • A group game at a home tournament, with the hosts playing elsewhere. The Netherlands were the defending champions.
  • Jonna Andersson put Sweden ahead before half-time. Jill Roord equalised seven minutes after it.
  • 21,342 attended — at the time a record crowd for a Women's Euro group match not involving the host nation. It lasted eight days: 22,596 watched the Netherlands beat Switzerland on the same pitch.
  • England beat Sweden 4–0 here in the semi-final on 26 July, in front of 28,624, and won the tournament at Wembley five days later.
  • The first women's football matches in Sheffield were played a few streets away in 1895, in front of a crowd of 3,000.

april 2023

  • Two second-half goals. Promotion to the Premier League with three games to spare.
  • In March 2002 the same fixture became the only match in the history of English professional football to be abandoned for want of players. United finished with six men, the score standing at 0–3, in front of 17,653.
  • Paul Heckingbottom had spent the build-up publicly asking supporters to stay off the pitch.
